G. Personnel - Planting and seeding shall be performed by experienced workmen familiar with planting procedures and under the supervision of a qualified foreman. The foreman shall be on the job site whenever planting is in progress.

H. Weather - No planting shall occur or will be permitted during weather conditions which will adversely affect materials, nor will it be permitted when soil is in a muddy condition.

I. All working plant material, not indicated for removal shall be protected in place, as necessary to ensure survival to the greatest extent possible.

## PART 2 - MATERIAL STANDARDS

A. Plant Materials - Plants shall be quality material having the habit of growth and/or to normal for the species, sound, vigorous, healthy, free from insects, plant diseases and injury. Concombrant land height and spread dimensions shall be the same as the land cover, but the American Standards for Nursery Stock and Antique Nursery Association (Annex) to Committee Recommended Average Tree Specifications. Plant names shall conform to those given in "Standardized Plant Names" listed earlier, proposed by the American Society of Plant Physicists and Engineers (ASSOCIATED). Nonstandard, or shall be names generally accepted by standards and good practice. Select, dig, transport, protest and plant in accordance with the requirements of these specifications and "General Standards for Nursery Stock" and "Standardized Plant Names". The use of these standards, in the Landscape Architect or Owner's representative will consider substitution of named equivalent size or variety if reasonable evidence is submitted that a plant is not obtainable. Container plants shall have been in the containers for a sufficient length of time for the rest system to hold the earth when taken from the container, but not long enough to become root-bound or cause "hardening off."

## PART 3 - SOIL PREPARATION

A. Topsoil - Topsoil, if required, shall be screened, fertile, thistle soil obtained from well-drained health tank, and shall be free from soil grass, willow, roots, leaves, etc. Soils, containers, soil or any material toxic to plant growth. Topsoil content shall be as follows: 0/8 (50-60%), Clay 10-20%, Sand 30-60%, with a minimum of 2% organic material (natural or added). pH shall not be lower than 9.5 nor exceed 9.3 and soluble salts shall not exceed 1800 ppm. Topsoil existing on site may be used if it meets the above specification.

B. Planting Pit - Planting pit backfill for all trees and shrubs shall be with approved native soil. Contractor shall stockpile native soil exsanded, and use same for backfilling planting soil.

C. Leam and Ground Cover Areas - Broadcast the following materials uniformly and thoroughly incorporate to a depth of 8° by means of a rotollier. Amount per 1000 Square Feet 3 cubic yards nitrogen stabilized organic amendment derived from rainwood sawdust, fir sawdust or finely ground bark. 10 pounds 16-20-5 Ammonium Phosphate

## PART 4 - LAWN PLANTING

A. Fire Coating - Fire grade by hand rating or dragging with an approved shift, removing all debris and color 1° in diameter and greater. True surface to finished grade, to be worn and firm at all points. Generally, finished grade shall be 1° to 1 1/2° below tops of adjacent curbs, walks, slabs, etc.

B. The Landscape Contractor will be responsible for providing the type of lawn specified on the plans, in accordance with the following specifications:

B.1. Worse lawn - October-April: Broadcast annual run grass at the rate of 10 feet/1800 sq. ft. and 10-4-15 fertilizer at 190 frame. Apply a top dressing of mulch at the rate of one cubic yard per 1000 sq. ft. Keep lawn areas moist until germination.

B.2. Summer lawn - May-September: Common bermuda. Hydroclutch common bermuda at the rate of 3 feet/1600 sq. ft. using 1750 lbs. fiber/acre. Keep lawn areas moist until germination.

B.3. Soil - Water saills and fine rake subgrade prior to taping soil. Place soil siltas parallel to each other, with staggered joints, and lay perpendicular to slopes when possible. Understand with high points. After placement, fertilize with ammonium phosphate (18-20-5) - 61 lbs/1000 sq. ft. and roll - using a 500 pound hand roller, water as required. Use a 1000 lb. per hauling soil growth.

C. Sprinkler Heads - Heads shall be raised to the proper heights as detailed prior to planting.

## PART 5 - EXECUTION

A. Grading - Bring all areas to finish grade after soil: prepping which shall be 3° to 3 1/2° for decomposed granite areas and 1° to 1 1/2° in lawn areas below paving and curb or as noted by spot abundance or details. Special attention shall be given to maintaining continuous and even finished. Door away from structures, and provide positive drainage to make and then surface. Earth terms shall take the form indicated on the plans. The Landscape Architect or Owner's representative shall review and approve all finished grades prior to any planting.

B. Trees, Shrubs, Ground Cover and Vines - Plants shall be subject to inspection and approval or rejection at place of growth or on the project site at any time before or during progress of work as to size, variety, condition, latent defects and injuries. Repeated plants shall be removed from the project site immediately at no cost to the owner.

C. Tree Layout - Layout tree locations with stakes or gypsum in locations shown on drawings. Control Landscape Architect or Owner's representative to check the proposed location of plants in the field. Adjustments may be made to determine exact positions before planting begins.

D. Pit Drainage - Test drainage of plant beds and pits by filling with water. Conditions causing the retention of water in planting beds for more than 24 hours shall be brought to the attention of the Landscape Architect or Owner's representative prior to any planting.

E. Obstruction - If rock, underground construction work, or other construction is encountered in the excavation of plant pits, alternative locations may be selected by the Landscape Architect or Owner's representative, but the same cannot be checked as determined by continuous Architect or Owner's representative, submit cost required to remove the obstruction to a depth as per plan detail. Proceed with work only after approval by Landscape Architect or Owner's representative.

F. Containers - Cut plant containers (cams) on two sides or slide plant out of can (do not pull plant out of the can by the truck only, use two hands, one on the truck and one under cart belt) without injuring rock ball. Do not cut container with spade. Any plant injured or root ball broken during planting operations will be rejected.

G. Plant Pits - Planting pits shall be backfilled with approved native soil and be water settled to a grade sufficient that, in the setting of the plant, the finish grade level after settlement will be the same as that at which the plants were grown (see details in landscape plans).

H. Ground Cover - All ground cover areas shall be treated with a pre-emergent as called out in granite areas.

H.1. Finish grade (excluding decomposed granite) in ground cover areas shall be 1° to 1.5° below tops of header, walks, etc.

H.2. Surrounding sidewalks and paving shall be kept brown clean daily during planting operation. Plant containers, empty fertilizer bags, seed bags and containers are to be zinked up and disposed of daily. Contractor's mark shall be removed from site at the completion of work.

H.3. Ground cover plants shall have lawn grass in flats or pots and shall remain in those flats until incorporated. At time of transplanting, the flat soil shall contain sufficient moisture so that the soil does not fall apart when tillting plants from the flat. Each plant shall be planted with its proportionate amount of flat soil in a manner that will insure a minimum disturbance to the rest system.

H.4. Poll material shall be planted to a sufficient depth to cover all roots. The earth around each plant shall be finned sufficiently to force out on pockets.

H.5. All ground cover planting shall be immediately watered to avoid drying out until the entire planted area is completed.

H.6. Immediately after planting, ground cover areas shall be top dressed with 1° of mulch.

I. Protection After Delivery

I.1. Upon delivery to the site, all nursery stock shall be painted as soon as possible. While awaiting planting, plants shall not be exposed to excessive sun or strong winds. Stock what is not satisfactory in the opinion of the Landscape Architect or Owner's representative shall be immediately replaced with acceptable stock.
